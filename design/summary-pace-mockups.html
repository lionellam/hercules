Spending pace on Summary: mockups

Review mockups for the current-month Summary. The aim is to show, for each budgeted category, whether spending so far is above or below what the budget allows by today, like a burn-up chart. Nothing in the app has changed.

Sample data: Saturday 7 November 2026, day 7 of 30. Budgets are Food & Dining $500, Transport $150, Groceries $400, Entertainment $100 and Rent $2,000. Shopping, Utilities and Health & Medical have no budget.

Expected by today = budget × day ÷ days in month, with today counted as a full day. On day 7 of 30, a $500 budget expects $116.67. "On pace" means within 5% of the budget either side of that figure.

Option A: pace marker on each bar, tap for the burn-up chart Recommended

The category list stays where it is. Each budgeted bar gains a small upright marker at "expected by today", with one line of text below saying whether you're over or under pace and how much a day you can still spend. Tapping a budgeted category opens its own burn-up chart in place. Only one opens at a time, so the screen doesn't get much longer.

In the expanded charts: solid line = what you've actually spent so far; dashed line = the budget spread evenly over the month; upright line = today. Hover or drag across a chart to see any day.

Option B: a grid of small burn-up charts

A new "Spending pace" section above the category list shows a small burn-up chart for every budgeted category. Each one is scaled to % of its own budget, so the dashed "expected" line is the same diagonal in every panel and they're easy to compare. It's the closest to what you described, but it adds a lot of height and the labels are tight on a phone.

Option C: over/under pace bars

A compact section where each budgeted category is a bar growing right (over pace) or left (under pace) from a centre "on pace" line, sized as a % of its budget. It's the quickest way to spot the problem categories, but it doesn't show how spending built up over the month. It also makes one-off payments like Rent look far over pace.

Questions for you

  1. Which option do you prefer: A, B, C, or a mix (for example A's markers plus B's grid on wider screens)?
  2. For the first 3 days of the month, should it hold back the over/under verdict (as shown in A on day 2)? Is within 5% of budget the right "on pace" band?
  3. Should budgeted categories with no spending yet appear in the list? This is shown here with Groceries at $0; Summary currently lists only categories with spending.
  4. Is the "$X left, about $Y a day" line useful, or should it show only the pace figure?
  5. Should categories without a budget open a chart too? It would show the running total only, or compared with last month by the same day.
  6. The previous-month tab would stay exactly as it is now, with no pace markers, since budgets only apply to the current month. Is that what you want?
