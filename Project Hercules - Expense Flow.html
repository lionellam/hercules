Logging an expense

This swimlane diagram shows what normally happens when someone logs an expense in Hercules, from typing it on the Log screen to seeing it saved.

Each column, or lane, belongs to one part of Hercules: the person using the app, the app on their phone (client side), the app online (server side), the database where expenses are stored, and Claude, the AI that reads what was typed. Read it from top to bottom. Each step sits in the lane of whoever does it, and the arrows show what happens next.

Amber notes show where the flow can stop. Purple notes show what changes when the experimental Intelligent Merchant Selection is switched on.

What's kept

Parsing doesn't save the expense; only Save expense does, along with the original typed text. For the daily limit, each parse is recorded with who made it and when, never the text.

What Claude sees

The typed text, the person's category names and today's date. With Intelligent Merchant Selection on, also the merchant names from this month's expenses, but not the rest of those expenses. Never the person's name or email.

Where each part runs

The Hercules app is one codebase in two sides: the client side runs in the browser on the person's phone, and the server side runs on Vercel. It relies on two outside services: the database (Postgres, hosted on Neon) and Claude Haiku, Anthropic's language model.
