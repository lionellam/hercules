HERCULES · USER GUIDE

How Hercules works

Hercules keeps track of what you spend. Type an expense the way you'd say it, like "lunch at Ya Kun 4.50", check what the app picked out, and save. Hercules adds it all up by day, month and category, and tells you how you're doing against any budgets you set.

Who it's for: invited family and friends Works on: phone, tablet and computer Updated: 13 November 2026

What Hercules is, and why

Hercules is a private expense tracker for family and close friends. Instead of filling in a form for every purchase, you type it the way you'd say it, such as "grab home 14.20 yesterday". Claude, an AI, picks out the amount, category, merchant and date. You check it and save. Hercules then keeps your spending organised by day, month and category, and tracks it against any monthly budgets you set.

Why it exists

Logging should take seconds

Most expense apps make you pick a category, type an amount, choose a date and fill in fields for every purchase. That friction is why expense logs fall behind. Hercules turns one line of everyday text into a complete record.

You stay in control

The AI only suggests. Nothing is saved until you've seen it and fixed anything that's wrong, and your original words are kept with every expense.

No subscription

Many budgeting apps charge a recurring fee. Hercules runs on free hosting, and the AI it uses costs a few cents a month for everyone combined, with a hard cap of US$5 a month. It's free for the people who use it.

Private by design

It's invite-only, each person sees only their own expenses, and the AI provider doesn't use what you type to train its models.

Budgets you can act on

Rather than only telling you at the end of the month that you overspent, Summary shows mid-month whether each budget is on pace, and roughly what you can spend a day for the rest of the month.

A test of a method

Hercules is also a real-world trial of CPMAI, a structured approach to running AI projects: use AI only where it genuinely helps, and keep everything else simple and predictable.

What it doesn't do

  • It doesn't connect to your bank or read receipts. You log each expense yourself.
  • It doesn't convert currencies. Totals are in your default currency (Singapore dollars unless you change it); spending in other currencies is recorded and shown separately.
  • Budgets are personal. There are no shared budgets between people.

Getting started

  1. Open the app at the link you were sent, and tap Sign in with Google. Use the Google account whose email address was invited. If you see "This account hasn't been invited", ask the app owner to add your address.
  2. Add it to your home screen so it opens like an app.
    iPhone (Safari): tap the Share button, then Add to Home Screen.
    Android (Chrome): tap the ⋮ menu, then Add to Home screen or Install app.
  3. Choose light or dark if you like. The app follows your phone's setting; to change that, go to Settings → Appearance.

You'll stay signed in on that device for up to 90 days. There are four tabs along the bottom: Log, History, Summary and Settings. On a tablet or computer the app gets wider and shows two columns.

Logging an expense

  1. Type the expense in the box at the top of the Log screen, in your own words.
  2. Tap Parse.AI Claude, the AI that reads your text, picks out the amount, category, merchant and date. This takes a second or two.
  3. Check the review card. Every field can be changed: tap the amount, pick a different category, fix the date, or add a remark.
  4. Tap Save. The expense appears straight away under Today, and in History and Summary.
  • Nothing is saved until you tap Save. If Claude got something wrong, just fix it on the card. There's no need to type it again.
  • Changed your mind? Tap Discard. Your typed text stays in the box so you can tweak it and parse again. If you've already changed something on the card, the app checks with you first.
  • Start again by tapping the × at the top right of the box, which clears what you've typed.
  • Spent today, under the box, shows your running total for the day. Below the Today list, Today by category breaks it down.
  • Offline? The app tells you, and keeps what you've typed until you're back online.
  • You can parse up to 100 expenses a day, which is plenty for normal use.

What to type

Write it the way you'd say it. Include the amount, and anything else that helps: where, what for, and when, if it wasn't today. For example:

  • Dates: no date means today. "Yesterday", "last Friday" and "3 Nov" all work.
  • Currency: your default currency (Singapore dollars unless you change it in Settings → Currency) unless you name another, such as "450 baht" or "USD 120". A plain "$" means your default. To change the currency before saving, tap the code beside the amount, such as SGD ▾. Totals count only your default currency; others are shown beside them as "Not included", without converting.
  • Categories: Claude can only choose from your own list. If nothing fits, it uses Other.
  • Remarks: extra details that don't fit elsewhere, such as "with the team", go into Remarks.

Fixing and deleting

Tap any expense, in the Today list or in History, to open it. Change what you need and tap Save. Expenses you've changed from what Claude suggested show a small edited tag.

To delete, open the expense and tap Delete. The app shows which expense it is and asks you to confirm. After deleting, an Undo bar appears at the bottom for about five seconds. Tap it to bring the expense back.

If you close an expense or category you've changed without saving, by tapping Cancel or tapping outside it, the app asks before throwing your changes away.

History

History lists every expense month by month, newest first, grouped by day. Use the arrows beside the month to go back to earlier months. Tap any expense to edit or delete it.

Searching. Type in the search box below the month to find expenses in that month. The list narrows with each letter, and shows how many match and what they add up to. You can search by merchant, category, the words you typed, remarks or amount, such as "grab" or "14.20". Tap × to clear it. Changing the month or leaving History clears the search too.

Summary

Summary shows this month and last month. Switch between them with the buttons at the top.

  • The total spent in the month. It stays at the top while you scroll, with the month buttons and CSV.
  • A bar for each category you've spent in, largest first. If the category has a budget, the bar fills up against it and reads, for example, "210.00 of 500.00". It turns red once you go over.
  • Share of spend, a pie chart of where the money went. With more than six categories, the smaller ones are grouped as "Everything else".
  • Spending pace, this month only, for categories with a budget. See the next section.
  • Download as a spreadsheet: tap CSV beside the total to save that month's expenses as a CSV file, which opens in Excel, Numbers or Google Sheets. This month gives everything so far; last month gives the whole month. On an iPhone with Hercules on the home screen, the share sheet opens: tap Save to Files, or send it by Mail or AirDrop.

Spending pace

Pace answers one question: am I spending faster than my budget allows? It assumes you spread each budget evenly over the month. With a $500 food budget in a 30-day month, that's about $16.67 a day, so by day 7 you'd expect to have spent about $116.67.

The small upright marker on each budgeted bar shows that expected amount for today. If your bar has gone past the marker, you're ahead of pace. The note under the bar tells you which of these applies:

NoteWhat it means
On paceYou're close to the expected amount, within 5% of the budget either way.
$X over paceYou've spent $X more than expected by today. You may want to slow down, but you're still within budget.
$X under paceYou've spent $X less than expected. There's room to spare.
Budget usedYou've spent exactly the budget, for example rent paid in full.
Over budget by $XYou've gone past the whole month's budget. This is the only note shown in red.

After the note you'll see what's left, and roughly how much a day you can spend for the rest of the month, for example "$290.00 left, about $12.61/day".

A big payment early in the month isn't overspending. If you pay all of your rent on the 1st, it shows "Budget used", not "over pace". Early in the month, a single purchase can also show as over pace, because only a few days' worth of budget is expected so far. It evens out as the month goes on.

The burn-up chart. Tap a budgeted category to open its chart. The solid line is your running total, stepping up on each day you spent. The dashed line is the budget spread evenly across the month, and the upright line is today. If the solid line is above the dashed one, you're over pace. Touch or drag across the chart to see any day's figures, or open Daily figures underneath for the same numbers in a table.

Settings

The Settings tab, with the gear icon, holds the things you set once and rarely change:

  • Categories & budgets: add, rename and retire categories, and set monthly budgets. See the next section.
  • Currency: your default currency, used whenever what you type doesn't name one. Choose from 20 common currencies; tap one and it's saved. Totals and budgets count only your default currency, so they're never a mix. Expenses in other currencies are still listed, with their code (like "USD 12.50"), and shown under the total as "Not included"; tap the ? beside it for a reminder why. Nothing is converted. Switching asks first; switching back restores your totals exactly. Changing it doesn't change past expenses.
  • Appearance: System follows your phone's light or dark setting; Light or Dark keeps the app that way. Your choice is remembered on that device.
  • Account: the email address you're signed in with, and Sign out, which asks you to confirm first.
  • Version: at the bottom, the app's version number and when it was last updated. Handy to mention if you report a problem.

Categories & budgets

Everyone starts with 14 categories, from Food & Dining to Travel, including Other. Go to Settings → Categories & budgets to:

  • Add a category: tap Add category, give it a name, and optionally a monthly budget.
  • Rename it or change its budget: tap the category. A new name shows on all its past expenses too.
  • Set a monthly budget in Singapore dollars, or leave it blank. Budgets turn on the "x of budget" bars and spending pace on Summary.
  • Retire a category you no longer use: open it and tap Retire. It stops being offered for new expenses, but past expenses keep it. After retiring, an Undo bar appears for a few seconds.
  • Bring one back: retired categories are listed under Retired at the bottom. Tap Restore. Adding a category with the same name as a retired one also restores it.

Other is the catch-all and can't be renamed or retired. You can have up to 50 categories in use.

Your privacy

Who can see your expenses

  • Only invited people can sign in, and each person sees only their own expenses.

What leaves the app

  • The text you type is sent to Claude (made by Anthropic) so it can read the expense, along with your list of category names. Anthropic doesn't use it to train its AI.
  • Signing in goes through Google, which tells the app your email address and name.
  • Your expenses are stored in the app's own database, not on your phone, and aren't shared with anyone else.

Deleting an expense removes it from everything you see. The only thing saved on your device is your light or dark choice.

Quick answers

Claude picked the wrong category or date.
Change it on the review card before you save. If it's already saved, tap the expense to edit it.
I wrote "tues" and it picked next Tuesday.
Check the date on the review card. Writing "last Tuesday" or the date itself, like "3 Nov", avoids this.
I can't find an expense I logged.
It's filed under its own date. In History, go to the month of that date. If it's dated a different day, it won't be in Today.
Can I get back an expense I deleted?
Only by tapping Undo in the few seconds after deleting. After that it can't be brought back in the app, so check before you confirm.
Why does a category say "over pace" on the 2nd of the month?
Only two days' worth of budget is expected so far, so one purchase can be ahead. It usually settles as the month goes on.
It says I've reached the daily limit.
You can parse 100 expenses in 24 hours. Try again later; anything you've typed stays in the box.
The app is slow to open after a while.
After a quiet spell the database takes a moment to wake up. It's quick again straight after.
